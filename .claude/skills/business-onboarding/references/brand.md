# Stage 1: 01-brand/brand.md

Everything about who the brand is: identity, what it sells, who it is for, how it is positioned, and approved messaging.

This is a menu, not a template. The file gets a section only for what the user actually gave you. Group and name sections to suit the brand; the groupings below are just a way to cover the ground.

Each item is marked:
- **Fact**: comes from the user or is left out. Never guess.
- **Judgment**: draw out with questions and options. `[AI guess, confirm]` allowed if they hand it to you.

## Core (needed before sign-off)

Cover these first. Each needs a specific, usable answer, not a generic one.

1. **Brand name.** Official spelling and capitalisation. **Fact.**
2. **What the brand is, in one line.** Max 20 words. Draft it from their answers and have them correct it.
3. **What they sell.** Every product and every service they want content about: what it is and who it is for. **Fact.** If they offer both products and services, cover both.
4. **Who it is for.** At least one specific customer: who they are, the main problem they bring, and at least one phrase they actually say. Anchor on "describe the one customer you wish you had a hundred more of." If they say "everyone", ask: "if you could only reach one group for a year, who?"
5. **What makes them different.** At least one differentiator a competitor honestly could not say, with a line of proof. Kill generic ones ("quality", "passion", "good service").
6. **Banned claims.** Ask directly: "is there anything you legally or factually must never say?" **Fact.** Always written: either the list with the reason for each (legal / unverified / off-brand), or "None stated by the user as of YYYY-MM-DD." This is what the pre-publish check screens against.

## Topics to explore (raise each at least once)

Bundle the smaller ones into a single question when it saves a turn. Keep what they give you; leave out what they do not have.

### Identity
- Accepted short forms of the name, and forms never to use. **Fact.**
- Mission (why it exists) and vision (where it is going). **Judgment.**
- Founding story: the problem that sparked it, a turning point, where it is now.
- Core values. Ask for a story, then name the value it reveals and let them correct the wording. Pick from the values bank in `examples.md` if they stall. Note how each shows up in content.
- Personality as "we are X, not Y". The contrast format is the unlock. Offer pairs from `examples.md`, then get one of their own.
- Quick facts: founded, location, markets, team size, website, social handles. **Fact.**

### What they sell (per product or service)
- Price, with the date it was last checked; billing and format for services (one-off, package, retainer, subscription). **Fact.**
- Where to buy, or how to enquire.
- Features, then benefits. You may draft "feature X means you get Y" from their features and have them confirm; that is rewording their input, not inventing.
- For services: what is and is not included, delivery timeline, the process from enquiry to delivery, typical results (verifiable only).
- Proof points: testimonials, reviews, awards, stats, each with a source. **Fact.**
- Content angles. Ask "what do customers get most excited about?" before suggesting any.
- Anything they must never claim about this specific product or service. **Fact.**
- Discontinued products or retired services worth keeping a record of, with the date.

### Audience
- More customer types, up to 4 in total, only if the user describes real distinct groups. Do not invent personas to reach a number.
- Per customer type: goals, frustrations, why they hesitate to buy, where they spend time online and when, preferred language(s), which products or services help them.
- The questions customers ask again and again, captured verbatim. These feed the FAQ and every educational pillar later.
- Who they are not trying to reach (anti-persona), and the signs content is drifting toward them.
- Where the audience picture comes from (real customer data, DMs, reviews, gut feel), so it can be reviewed later.

### Positioning and competitors
- Positioning statement, filled together: "For [audience], [brand] is the [category] that [differentiator], unlike [alternative]." Offer shapes from the positioning bank in `examples.md`.
- Category, and whether they are trying to create or redefine one.
- More differentiators, up to 5, each with proof.
- Price position (budget, mid, premium) and why.
- Competitors, up to 5: who they are, their strength, their weakness, how this brand differs, their social presence, what to learn from them.
- Whether naming competitors in content is allowed, and any verified comparison claims.

### Messages and FAQ
- The one core message every post ladders up to.
- 2 to 3 supporting messages, each with proof.
- Elevator pitch, in whichever lengths they will use.
- Approved claims, verified true, with source and date. **Fact.**
- FAQ: answers to the questions captured under Audience, in brand voice, with where to send the person next.
- Holding statements for complaints (delivery issues, service complaints, anything to escalate) and who to escalate to.
