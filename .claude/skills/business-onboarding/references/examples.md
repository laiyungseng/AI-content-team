# Option banks

Pull from these when the user stalls. Show 4 to 6 options, ask which is closest and what is wrong with it, then get one of their own. Never present a bank as the answer.

## Personality pairs ("we are X, not Y")

- Warm, not corporate
- Direct, not blunt
- Playful, not silly
- Expert, not lecturing
- Calm, not sleepy
- Bold, not arrogant
- Practical, not boring
- Premium, not stiff
- Honest, not brutal
- Friendly, not chummy

## Core values (as behaviours, not words)

- We say the price upfront, even when it loses the sale
- We tell customers when they do not need our product
- We answer every message the same day
- We fix our mistakes before anyone has to ask
- We only sell what we would use ourselves
- We explain the why, not just the what
- We name the trade-off instead of hiding it

## Positioning shapes

- The affordable option that does not feel cheap
- The specialist for one narrow group everyone else treats as an afterthought
- The one that removes a specific step everyone assumes is unavoidable
- The most transparent option in an opaque category
- The fastest, where speed is the actual pain
- The one that teaches you to do it yourself

## Audience frustrations to test against

- Nobody explains it in plain language
- Every option looks the same and I cannot compare
- I got burned last time and do not trust the category
- It takes too long and I need it now
- I do not know if I am being overcharged
- I am embarrassed to ask a basic question

## Voice sample contrast pair

Show both, ask which sounds like them and what is wrong with the other.

**A:** "New drop is live. Three colours, limited run. Get yours before it's gone."

**B:** "We made 40 of these. That's it. If you've been waiting on the green one, this is the moment."

Then a second pair in a different register (formal vs casual, funny vs plain) based on what they picked.

## Tone by context, sample lines

- Launch: excited but specific, name the one thing that is new
- Educational: calm, one idea per post, no selling
- Promo: direct about price and deadline, no hype adjectives
- Trending: light, quick, only if it fits the brand
- Complaint reply: acknowledge, take it to DM, no defensiveness

## Hook patterns

- The mistake: "Most people [common action]. That's why [bad outcome]."
- The number: "3 things I'd do differently if I started [X] today"
- The contradiction: "Everyone says [belief]. Here's what actually happens."
- The question: "How much should [thing] actually cost?"
- The stakes: "This one line in your [contract/caption/recipe] costs you [X]."
- The story open: "A customer called me at 11pm about..."
- The demo: "Watch what happens when..."
- The confession: "We got this wrong for two years."
- The comparison: "[Option A] vs [Option B], honestly."
- The shortcut: "The 30-second version of [complicated thing]."

## CTA bank

**Engagement:** "Which one would you pick?" / "Save this for when you need it" / "Tag someone who needs to see this" / "Tell me if I'm wrong"

**Traffic:** "Full breakdown at the link in bio" / "Link in bio if you want the long version"

**Product:** "Link in bio to order" / "In stock now, link in bio" / "DM the word [X] and we'll send it over"

**Service:** "DM us to check availability" / "Book a free 15-minute call, link in bio" / "Comment [word] and we'll send details"

**Rotation rule default:** never the same CTA twice in a row; roughly 2 engagement CTAs for every 1 sales CTA.

## Visual look starting points

- Clean and bright: white background, natural light, lots of space, one subject
- Warm documentary: real people mid-action, slightly grainy, warm grade
- Bold graphic: flat colour blocks, big type, high contrast
- Dark premium: low key lighting, deep shadows, one accent colour
- Handmade: texture, imperfect edges, visible process
