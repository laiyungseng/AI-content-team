# ad_copy_longform

**Purpose:** write long-form post and ad copy (Facebook-style, 400 to 1800 words) that carries a reader from the problem to a clear action. Used for the caption or body of a static post, an ad, or a long caption under a carousel or reel. Hooks come from `shared/frameworks/hooks.md`, CTAs from `shared/frameworks/cta.md`.

The method is the skeleton. **Brand voice, market, language and claims come from `brand/[project]/`**, not from this file. Everything about idiom and emoji below is an example of a market style, not a rule for every brand.

## Rules
1. Pure executor of the brief: the angle, offer, platform and number of variants come from the approved brief. Missing one of the five inputs below: stop and ask; do not guess.
2. Do not pull a "menu" of selling points out of the brand files for the user to choose; the main push of this piece is whatever the brief says.
3. N variants need N different hooks. Never repeat a hook type to save effort.
4. Every variant passes the six-element check (below) before it is shown.
5. Never write back to `brand/`. Never publish. Copy goes to `drafts/` for `content_reviewer`.
6. Write in the language the brief names. Claims follow `brand/[project]/prohibited_claims.md` and `shared/platform_rules/meta.md`.

## Inputs (all five)
1. Brand files for the project (voice, audience, offer, proof, prohibited claims).
2. What this piece pushes (from the brief; the user, not you, decides).
3. Platform (changes emoji use, length, CTA style).
4. Number of variants (default 5).
5. Length (default medium; industry bands below, or match a sample the user supplies).
Optional: a sample to use as extra style reference (the skeleton still applies) and emoji on or off. If the user supplies a sample, ask once whether to keep its style notes (length range, sentence pattern, special markers) and where.

## Six-section skeleton
Sections are separated by a line holding a single `.` (a common long-form Facebook habit; use blank lines where the platform does not need it).

1. **Hook** (1 to 5 lines): stop the scroll. See hook choice below.
2. **Pain** (the Issue): 3 to 5 bullet pains (❌) or a short story/question that makes the reader feel it.
3. **Turn, Solution, Knowledge:** a turn line ("the real problem is..."), the solution (✅ list), then 3 to 5 steps or method points (📌), each with 2 to 3 lines of substance, not just a title.
4. **Certified proof:** credentials, years, cases, numbers, certifications. Only what is true and in the brand files.
5. **Scarcity and offer:** why act now, the offer with dates or quantity, price against price when real.
6. **CTA, contact, hashtags:** primary channel first, then others; address for physical services; hashtags last.

## Six-element check (self-check each variant; add what is missing)
| Element | Meaning | Usually lives in |
|---|---|---|
| S Solution | says what fixes it | section 3 |
| N Numbers | at least one concrete figure (amount, years, people, percent, multiple) | spread through |
| I Issue | the sharpest pain is described | section 2 |
| C Certified proof | credential, case count, award, certification | section 4 |
| K Knowledge | teaches something (steps, how, why) | section 3 |
| .C Scarcity **and** CTA | both a reason to act now and an explicit action; not one or the other | sections 5 and 6 |

Weave the elements into the copy. Never print labels like `[S]` or `[N]` in the text. Which elements to give most room depends on the business: courses and training lean on proof then knowledge; health lean on knowledge, proof, then the pain; property and investment lean on numbers then solution; services (finance, renovation) lean on proof then process; food and products on numbers then scarcity; B2B finance on proof then process.

## Hook choice
Use the hook types in `shared/frameworks/hooks.md`; name the type per variant. Default weighting from the source analysis of 40 successful long-form ads: pain-question about 45% of use (weight x4, with empathy openers), number shock and result-first about 15% to 17% each (x2), story, secret reveal and contrast about 10% each (x1.5), authority x1, others x1. Adjust by business: authority up for supplements and B2B courses; numbers up for property and investment; benefit-direct and price comparison up for food. Weighting is a starting hypothesis; once the project has its own winning hooks (memory, hook analysis), those outweigh this table.

A five-variant set usually reads: one pain-question, one number or result-first, one story or secret, one contrast or authority, one other.

## Markers
Visual markers at the start of lines help scanning:
- ❌ pains (3 to 5; more tires the reader) · ✅ solutions · 📌 numbered steps · 🏆 credentials · 🔥 urgency · 📲 contact · 📍 address · 💡 turn.
- Multi-item lists (packages, services) may use a coloured emoji per row.
- Not in the middle of body paragraphs, and not in the hashtag block. If the brand voice says no emoji, replace markers with letters (A, B, C) or plain bullets.
- A CTA **button line** carries no icon emoji ("Tap Send Message to order", not "📩 Tap Send Message to order"); contact info lines may carry one.

## Style notes (market example, Malaysian Chinese mixed with English)
Take these as a worked example of a market style; for another market write the equivalent rules into `brand/[project]/brand_voice.md`.
1. English only for nouns and adjectives that people really say in English (customer, course, ROI); verbs and verb phrases stay in the main language.
2. Use the local colloquial markers and particles of the market, sparingly; filler particles at most two or three per piece.
3. Rhythm of three: a question that lets the reader finish the answer, a flat statement of the truth, then an action. Mix question marks and exclamation marks; do not end every line with a full stop.
4. Medium sentences (about 12 to 25 characters or 10 to 20 words; occasionally longer for detail). Never over 50 characters in one sentence.
5. Core nouns like "copy" or "caption" use the local word, not a borrowed English word.

## Length bands (words or characters as the language counts them)
Course or seminar 1200 to 1600 · product sales 1200 to 1800 · property or investment 1000 to 1500 · home renovation 1000 to 1200 · finance 900 to 1300 · travel 800 to 1200 · medical or beauty 600 to 1000 · food 400 to 800. Default 800 to 1200 when unknown. Short version 200 to 400 (single carousel caption, broadcast); long 1500+ (seminar, high-ticket). The user can override.

## CTA block and hashtags
Order: urgency line ("limited places, first come first served") → specific offer ("first 20 get X", "was RM X, now RM Y", "bonus worth RM X") → channels (primary WhatsApp or chat link; URL; phone; address for physical services) → hashtags. Link types and conversion order: `shared/frameworks/cta.md`.

Hashtag mix: brand 15% · product or service keywords 35% · location 20% · industry terms 15% · long-tail search phrases 10% · community or event terms 5%. Count: courses 8 to 15, property or product 8 to 12, services 8 to 10, food 6 to 8.

## Output
Per variant, ready to paste: `Variant N`, the hook type on its own line (metadata, not in the copy), the copy, hashtags last. No developer notes or sample references inside the copy. Written to `drafts/<content id>/copy_vN.md`; statuses and approval as in [[carousel_generation]] step 7.

## Review checks (for `content_reviewer`)
- All six elements present; scarcity and CTA both.
- N different hook types across N variants.
- Every number, credential and certification is supported by the brand files; no claim in `prohibited_claims.md`; regulated wording per `meta.md`.
- Language and register match the brand voice; no stacked filler particles; no single sentence over 50 characters.
- Contact block complete; CTA button line without an icon.
- Reads as copy-ready, with no leftover labels.

## Failure handling
| Situation | Do |
|---|---|
| "Write me an ad" with no brief | Stop; ask which brief or push |
| Selling point not given | Stop and ask; do not extract one from the brand files |
| "Use the same hook five times" | Decline: use five hooks, or one variant |
| Too many filler particles or slang | Re-read style note 2; rewrite |
| Language outside the brand's markets | Check `brand/[project]/`; write in the language the brief names, or stop |
