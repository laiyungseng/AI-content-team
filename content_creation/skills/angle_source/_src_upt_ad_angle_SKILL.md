# [brand] Ad Angle

## Mission

Produce ad angles that [brand] can realistically test tomorrow.

Do not only brainstorm. Diagnose the market, find buyer hesitation, kill weak ideas, rewrite average ideas, score strictly, and select the best 3 concepts for creative testing.

## Load References

Always read the relevant reference files before producing a final answer:

- `references/brand/[brand]-marketing.md` for [brand] positioning.
- `references/brand/language-style.md` when the output is Chinese, Malaysian Chinese, or mixed MY-CN.
- `references/brand/offer-positioning.md` when the offer is [brand], AI training, marketing service, or agency-like.
- `references/rubrics/brutal-evaluation.md` and `references/rubrics/weak-idea-kill-filter.md` before scoring.
- `references/rubrics/generic-output-detector.md` before final delivery.
- `references/compliance/sensitive-claims.md` for regulated or risky industries.

Use `references/examples/golden-examples.md` and `references/examples/bad-examples.md` when the brief is close to home/custom, lead quality, AI course, or financing.

## Workflow

Follow this process every time:

1. State assumptions.
2. Diagnose the business: what is sold, who buys, what the buyer believes, what they worry about, why they delay or reject, who should be attracted, and who should be filtered out.
3. Analyze market pressure. Explain how price war, cheap competitors, low-quality leads, audience fatigue, trust issues, commoditization, or platform changes should change the message strategy.
4. Mine pain points across surface, hidden, financial, time, trust, status, operational, and market-pressure pains.
5. Build the buyer hesitation map. For each hesitation, show what the buyer says, what they actually mean, why it blocks purchase, and how the ad should handle it.
6. Cluster keywords by industry, product/service, pain, hesitation, result, comparison, urgency, proof, Malaysia context, wrong-fit customer, and high-intent customer.
7. Generate 12 meaningfully different initial ad angles.
8. Run the weak idea kill filter. Mark each angle as Keep, Rewrite, or Kill.
9. Rewrite all salvageable weak ideas with sharper specificity, buyer insight, contrast, sales logic, and visual clarity.
10. Generate 3 hooks each for the top 5 angles: direct pain, contrarian, and curiosity.
11. Score every final angle out of 50 using the brutal scorecard.
12. Select the best 3 concepts to test. They must be strategically different, not 3 wordings of the same idea.
13. Give creative direction for each top concept: format, visual scene, who appears, what is shown, on-screen text, tone, what to avoid, ratio, and platform.
14. Add compliance and risk notes.
15. Recommend the next test.

## Quality Bar

Before final delivery, reject or rewrite anything that:

- Could apply to any industry.
- Does not mention buyer hesitation.
- Does not show market pressure.
- Has no contrast.
- Uses slogans instead of hooks.
- Repeats the same angle in different wording.
- Has no sales logic.
- Has no visual execution idea.
- Has no reason why the angle could work.
- Has no risk warning.
- Sounds like an agency proposal or translated English.

Success standard: [brand] can take at least 3 concepts from the output and create ads tomorrow.
