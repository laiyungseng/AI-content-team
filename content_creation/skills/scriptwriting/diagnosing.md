# Diagnosing a Script or a Flop

Load this when something already exists and the job is to find what is wrong with it: a video that underperformed, a script the user wants feedback on, or content getting views without generating business.

The method is the same in all three cases. Find the symptom, name the cause, then load only the file that fixes it. Diagnosing is a narrowing exercise, so resist opening every reference at once.

## 1. Symptom to cause

Where the viewer leaves tells you which part of the script broke.

| Symptom | Cause | Where to fix it |
|---|---|---|
| Churn in the first 3 seconds | The hook | `writing-a-hook.md` |
| Churn at 8 to 15 seconds | Speed to value. The payoff was signalled but not delivered fast enough | `psychology.md` section 5 |
| Confused early, comments asking what this is even about | The funnel was skipped. Tactics arrived before context and stakes | `psychology.md` section 1 |
| Slow bleed throughout | One of three: no contrast words re-hooking in the body, value density (tangents), or rhythm (every sentence the same length) | `writing-a-script.md` section 4 for re-hooks, `psychology.md` sections 6 and 12 for density and rhythm |
| Watched but no saves, no shares, no action | Comprehension or absorption. Points did not land simply enough, or the action is too far away | `psychology.md` sections 7 and 9 |
| Watched to the end but feels unsatisfying | The curiosity loop never closed. The question popped in the hook was never visibly answered | `writing-a-script.md` section 5 |
| Flat response despite clear points | Emotional resonance. No emotion was chosen and screened for | `psychology.md` section 11 |
| No single moment worth remembering | No contrast. Nothing in the script is a claim anyone would disagree with | `writing-a-script.md` section 2 |
| Good views, no leads | CTA problem | `ctas.md` |

If the user has retention data, the drop-off timestamp picks the row for you. If they do not, ask where they think people leave, or read the script and predict it.

## 2. The seven recurring hook failures

Nearly every flopped hook maps to one or more of these. They are worth checking directly, because naming the pattern is more useful to a creator than a general note that the hook was weak. `hook-examples.md` has real paired examples of each with links.

1. **Wasted real estate.** "In my opinion", "What if I told you", "Guys, you're never going to believe this." Filler in the highest-value seconds.
2. **"Me" framing.** The viewer has to decide whether they see themselves in you. That pause kills reflexive opt-in. Exception: when the premise is so relatable that "I" reads as "everyone".
3. **No contrast.** A claim nobody would disagree with. No baseline, no comparison, no curiosity.
4. **Misalignment.** Spoken says one thing, text says another, visual shows a third.
5. **Disconnected points.** Multiple facts that only cohere after watching. At the hook stage they read as confusion.
6. **Visual undersell.** Showing proof that is not impressive, or flashing the key visual too briefly to register.
7. **Wrong sequencing.** Question in text, answer in speech, forcing the viewer to read before the words make sense.

## 3. Reviewing a script that already exists

Work in this order, because a problem found early makes the later notes irrelevant. There is no point tightening body rhythm in a script whose premise has no contrast.

1. **Is there a contrarian take at all?** Write down what the audience believes and what this script claims instead. If they are the same, that is the entire note. Stop and say so.
2. **Does the hook pop one clear question, and does the body answer that exact question?** A mismatch here can survive an otherwise well-written script, so check it explicitly rather than assuming good writing means good structure.
3. **Run the seven hook failures** in section 2 against the opening.
4. **Check the body connections.** Does each line follow from the one before it, or does the viewer have to jump a gap?
5. **Check for re-hooks.** Are there contrast words reopening loops through the middle, or does the body run flat after the opening?
6. **Check the payoff.** Is the loop visibly closed, or does it just stop?
7. **Check the CTA.** Present at all? Lead CTA where one is available? Placed where the flow allows?
8. **Then, and only then, line edits.** Reading level, sentence length variety, cut anything the viewer would not pay for.

Give the notes in that order too. Leading a review with line edits when the premise is flat tells the creator the script was nearly there when it was not.

## 4. When the diagnosis is the premise

Sometimes the honest answer is that the video was well made and the idea was not interesting. That is a real diagnosis and worth saying directly, because the alternative is the creator rewording hooks on a topic that was never going to perform.

Say it plainly, then help find a real angle: what do they believe about this topic that their audience does not, and can they prove it? If nothing surfaces, recommend a different topic rather than another draft.
