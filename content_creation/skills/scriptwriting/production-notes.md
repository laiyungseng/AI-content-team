# Production Notes

Everything about turning a written script or hook into a shot, cut video: casting, delivery, on-screen type, visual build, cutting pace, and the playback tests.

This is separated from the writing files on purpose. A request for a script should come back as a script, not as opinions about font size. Load this when the user is about to shoot or edit, when they ask why a hook that reads well is not working on screen, or when a delivered script needs hand-off notes so the edit does not undo the alignment the writing established.

## 1. Casting and delivery

**Who should be on screen.** To build personal brand value for one individual, that person should be on screen as much as possible without losing focus. In a vacuum, pick the most attractive and most relatable person available for the context. A ripped fitness creator outperforms one who is not.

**Delivery:**
- Speak with good pace and energy. Even a naturally monotone speaker should push energy up on camera to lift pace and excitement. It signals that the creator actually wants to be talking about this.
- Timbre helps. A naturally punchy, deep or sharp voice cuts through with less effort. A soft or flat voice needs extra energy in the delivery to compensate.
- Clean, high-quality audio. Heavy echo or a low monotone voice hurts performance.
- **Enunciation.** Pronounce syllables clearly, without mumbling or over-dramatising. Viewers will not decode a stumbled line, they leave.
- If you trip on a word, do it again. Shoot multiple takes, especially when the visual hook is creator a-roll only.

## 2. On-screen text

The text hook is the highest-weighted component of a hook, so the type treatment is not a cosmetic decision.

- Fewest words possible
- Font as large as possible without overwhelming the screen
- Use a very readable font
- Easy to read over the visuals. Cheat code: put a white or colored background behind it
- Keep it close to the subject's eye level without blocking anything important
- Make sure platform overlays do not cover it, and that it is visible in the feed preview

A text hook that is technically perfect but unreadable in the feed preview has failed before anyone heard the spoken line.

## 3. Building the visual

Enough motion and color to capture attention. A static black and white image will not hold anyone. The brain reacts subconsciously to color and motion.

**If using creator a-roll:**
- Looking attractive and happy helps
- Include initial motion or passive action while delivering the line
- **Initial motion:** starting close to camera then leaning away slightly — https://www.instagram.com/reel/CxwM39_vRKe/
- **Passive action ("Pope in the Pool"):** doing something passively while explaining something else, like doing makeup while talking. The action holds base attention so people stay focused — https://www.tiktok.com/@alixearle/video/7502591677652159775

**If using other b-roll:**
- Direct attention where it needs to go using arrows, circles, or color — https://www.instagram.com/reel/DJcAEnPRqx9/
- Switch visuals every 1 to 2 seconds. Each change stuns the viewer into a 10 to 20ms reset. Too aggressive and they get visually confused and bounce. Find the sweet spot.
- High contrast, high color, high motion wins

## 4. Cutting the hook

Assume all viewers' eyes can only look at one spot at a time. Have the visuals and text been placed so they see what they need to, in the order intended?

Two things to watch for:
- **Comprehension via alignment.** Do all four components feel aligned, easy to understand, and create a curiosity loop?
- **Pacing.** Is the spoken speed plus visual switching fast enough to hold attention?

Alignment can be specified correctly in the writing and still be lost here. All four components can be right on the page while the cut puts the text on screen after the spoken line has already moved past it.

## 5. The playback tests

**The three-pass test.** Run every hook through all three:
1. Watch on silent. Is the topic and curiosity loop clear?
2. Listen with eyes closed. Is the topic and curiosity loop clear?
3. Watch and listen together. Is the topic and curiosity loop clear?

Pass all three and the hook is sound.

**The honest gut check.** On playback, ask:
- Are you hooked? If not, why would they be?
- Are you clear on what the video is about?
- Are you curious enough to keep watching?
- Are you bored or locked in?

## 6. Tools

- Text to image: Midjourney, ChatGPT image generation
- Text to video: Google Veo, Krea AI, Runway ML
- Hook A/B testing: Instagram Trial Reels, for running 4 variants against each other
- Feed sorting for research: see `hook-research.md`
