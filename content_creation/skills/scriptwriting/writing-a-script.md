# Writing a Full Script

The end-to-end process for building a script, from contrarian take to finished draft. Load this for any full-script request.

Pair it with `formats.md`, which supplies the shape of the body, and `ctas.md`, which supplies the close. Those two are lookup files, so read the relevant rows rather than the whole thing. Hook craft lives in `writing-a-hook.md` and only needs loading when the hook itself is the hard part.

## 1. The core formula

Every script, in every format, sits on the same base structure. Only the body changes between formats.

| Section | Length (short form) | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | First 1 to 3 lines | Pop a question into the viewer's mind using a contrarian take framed with contrast |
| Body / buildup | Next 8 to 10 lines | Deliver the facts, opinions, examples and visuals that prove the hook's claim, with head fakes (mini re-hooks) along the way |
| Payoff + CTA | Final lines | Close the curiosity loop, land the emotional payoff, then ask for the action |

Most short-form videos run 45 to 60 seconds. That is room for two to three buckets of substance, no more. The common shape is five parts: hook, three body pieces, CTA.

The story structure that changes between formats mostly affects the body. Hook and CTA stay structurally similar across all formats. Pick the format first, then fill the body with that format's shape.

## 2. The workflow

1. **Pick the format.** This decides the story structure for the body. See `formats.md`.
2. **Establish the contrarian take, premise or angle.** What do you believe that this audience does not?
3. **Gather evidence.** Facts, opinions, visuals, examples that support the claim.
4. **Identify the most interesting substance.** Discard the rest. Two to three buckets is the ceiling.
5. **Define the contrast explicitly.** Write down the common belief, then write down your reality next to it. If they are not meaningfully different, stop and go back to step 2.
6. **Write the script.** Hook, then body in the format's shape, then payoff and CTA.

The structure is flexible. Not every step has to run in order.

**Where the time goes.** Performance is driven mostly by how much contrast exists, not by how polished the writing is. Most of the effort belongs in steps 2 and 5, not in rewording lines.

The take itself can come from either of two places: genuinely new information, or a different angle on information the audience already has. The second is the one people overlook, and it is much more available than the first.

**Reverse-prompt to find the take.** Ask: what is the single most interesting or valuable thing about this topic? What do I believe that most people in this audience do not? A useful exercise is to explain the story out loud to a voice assistant and ask what it found most interesting. That filters the noise and surfaces the real insight.

**No contrast, no video.** If there is no compelling contrast, the video will not perform. It will not be interesting enough. Say so plainly rather than writing a competent script around a dull premise. Offering two or three candidate angles is more useful than delivering a polished script on a flat one.

## 3. The hook, structurally

For full hook craft, load `writing-a-hook.md`. Structurally, all the hook has to do in the script is frame the core premise as a contrarian take.

**The gut check that matters:** when the viewer reads or hears the hook, what exact question pops into their head? Write that question down. If it is vague, or if it is not the question the body actually answers, the hook is wrong. Rewrite it before touching anything else.

## 4. Writing the body

Think of the script as a courtroom argument. The hook is the claim. The body is the evidence that convinces the jury that the claim is true.

**Rule 1: minimise the distance between sentences.**
Each new fact should connect as tightly as possible to the sentence directly before it, so the brain never has to jump a logical gap. Ask three questions in sequence: what did I just say, what would that make someone think next, what is the closest piece of evidence I have that answers that thought?

**Rule 2: embed contrast inside the body, not just in the hook.**
Contrast words are mini re-hooks: "but actually", "however", "the truth is", "and even though that was true". Each one reopens a curiosity loop mid-video, which is what stops the slow bleed at 15 to 30 seconds.

> Example: "NASA just invented a rocket that reaches the moon in 60 seconds. Most people thought this was impossible, but actually they are using a new fuel that burns 5,000 times hotter."

**Rule 3: pick shocking but provable facts.**
A wild claim with no evidence makes the creator look unreliable and burns trust. A wild claim with evidence attached is the most compelling content available. If a claim cannot be proven in one line, cut it or find proof.

**Rule 4: give context and stakes before tactics.**
The brain consumes information top down. Tactics that arrive before the viewer has a container to put them in feel like noise, and they churn. `psychology.md` section 1 has the full funnel.

**Rule 5: treat every sentence as if it costs $100.**
Would the viewer pay for this line? If not, delete it. Fewest possible words while staying clear.

**Rule 6: signal proof early, as a casual aside.**
A credibility line usually belongs within the first few lines of the body. Drop it and keep moving. Never a boast.

## 5. Writing the payoff

The payoff does two jobs, in this order:

1. **Close the loop.** Reveal the "so what". The final statement, the takeaway, or a recap of the principle extracted from the example. The question popped in the hook must be visibly answered.
2. **Convert.** Ask for the action immediately after that dopamine release, while the viewer is still in the payoff. See `ctas.md`.

A script that never closes its loop feels like a rug pull, and viewers punish it with lower saves, shares and follows even if they watched to the end.

Every script gets a CTA, and a lead CTA beats a follow CTA whenever one is available. Place it where the story flow allows, not wherever is convenient.

## 6. Knowing when a script is done

Four questions. Any "no" sends the script back:

- Is it clear?
- Is it interesting?
- Can you follow the line of logic from start to end?
- Do you actually believe the contrarian take?

Two mechanical checks worth running at the same time:

- **Reading level.** Sixth grade or below. Short sentences, active voice.
- **The jagged edge.** Look at the right-hand edge of the script. Varied line lengths make a jagged edge, which reads as natural speech. A straight edge means every sentence is the same length and it will land as background noise.

## 7. Writing a script from an existing transcript

Modelling a proven video is the fastest way to improve. Two paths: handwrite by tweaking the transcript, or work through it conversationally with an AI tool.

**The process:**

1. Take the transcript of a video whose format and story structure are worth replicating.
2. Break down its story structure: problem, solution, hook, credibility line, body substance buckets, CTA.
3. Generate several contrarian insight options that could replace the original angle.
4. Pick the most compelling one, or supply a better one from personal experience.
5. Remap the script around the chosen insight while staying as close as possible to the proven original structure.
6. Iterate line by line. Flag anything that does not sound right, rewrite, tighten.

**The known failure mode:** it is hardest to inject a strong contrarian insight into someone else's topic when you have no firsthand experience with it. The contrarian insight is the idea sauce. Without real experience or proof (results, data, a track record) behind the new angle, the rewrite tends to underperform the original. When a user is modelling a topic they have not lived, flag this rather than producing a confident script on a hollow premise.

**When comparing a draft against a proven video,** the differences almost always reduce to three things:
- How they framed the contrarian take in the hook
- Which body substance they chose, and in what order
- How they embedded contrast in the body to keep re-hooking

## 8. Assembly checklist

| Step | What to nail down |
|---|---|
| 1. Format | Which format fits, educational or storytelling |
| 2. Contrarian take | What you believe that most people do not, and whether you actually believe it |
| 3. Hook | First 1 to 3 lines, frames the take, pops one clear question |
| 4. Body | 2 to 3 substance buckets, each connected to the line before, contrast embedded throughout |
| 5. Payoff | Curiosity loop closed, the "so what" delivered |
| 6. CTA | Lead magnet CTA preferred, embedded in the natural flow |
| 7. Quality check | Clear, interesting, logical, believed |
