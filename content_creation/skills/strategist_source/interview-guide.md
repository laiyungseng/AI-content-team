# Interview Guide

Question bank per phase. Select 2 to 3 questions per turn, adapt wording to the user's context, and use the probes when answers come back vague. "If unsure" entries are defaults to propose when the user cannot answer; always present them as proposals to confirm, never as facts.

## Phase 0: Setup and read the brand

Do not open with questions. Open by reading `01-brand/brand.md` (and `02-guidelines/voice-and-rules.md` if present), then reflect back what you found.

- Read `brand.md`. Summarize the business, products/services, personas, positioning, and core message in 2 to 4 sentences.
- "Here's what I picked up from your brand file: [summary]. Anything off before we build strategy on top of it?"
- `brand.md` only holds sections the user actually gave. A missing section means unknown, not "none".
- If `brand.md` is still `status: skeleton` or too thin to build on: "Your brand file isn't filled in yet. The strategy will be far stronger if we do that first with the initial-brand-folder-setup skill. Want to pause and do that, or should I grab just the essentials now and come back to it?"

Set expectations: "Strategy is the deepest part of this system, so this is a real conversation, usually 15 to 25 exchanges. I'll ask a few questions at a time, and by the end you'll have a complete strategy and 3 to 5 content pillars in strategy.md. Say 'propose it for me' anytime and I'll draft answers for you to correct."

## Phase 1: Confirm the foundation

You already have the basics from `brand.md`. Only ask what the brand file doesn't cover:

- "If we're celebrating in 12 months, what happened? Be concrete: revenue, clients, launches." (This forward-looking goal is usually NOT in brand.md; it's what social objectives descend from.)
- "Why does social media matter for this business right now? What's it supposed to do that isn't happening?"
- Only if `brand.md` didn't make it clear: "Which revenue stream matters most, and which do you want social to push hardest?"

If unsure about the goal: propose the most common objective for their business type (local service: booked calls/DMs; e-commerce: traffic and repeat purchase; personal brand: audience growth and inbound leads) and ask them to react.

## Phase 2: Audience angle for content

Personas come from `brand.md`; do not rebuild them. Sharpen only what pillars need:

- "What questions do customers ask you again and again, before and after buying?" (Gold for the Educate pillar. Write these verbatim. Skip if brand.md already captured them.)
- "For each of your personas, where do they actually spend time online and what do they consume there?"

Probes:
- If personas are thin in brand.md: flag it and suggest strengthening brand.md later, but proceed with what you have.
- B2B: "Who feels the pain, who signs the check, and who researches? Which one does your content need to reach?"

## Phase 3: Current state

- "What have you posted so far? What's the one post that did surprisingly well, and the one that flopped?"
- "Which accounts in or near your space do you admire? What do they do that you'd steal?"
- "Who are your 2 or 3 real competitors, and what are they doing on social?"
- "Honestly: how many posts per week can you sustain, in what formats, and who makes them?" (Anchor everything to this. Revisit it in Phase 7.)

Probes:
- Nothing posted yet: skip audit questions, spend the time on admired accounts instead.
- Capacity optimism ("10 posts a week, easy"): "What's the most you've actually sustained for a month straight?"

If unsure about what worked: proceed without it, and note in the final document that the first 90 days double as a data-gathering period.

## Phase 4: Objectives, funnel, and platforms

- "Given the business goal from Phase 1, here's my draft social objective: [verb + metric + target + deadline]. What would you change?"
- "When a stranger discovers you, what should make them stop scrolling? When they're deciding whether to trust you, what convinces them? When they're ready to buy, what pushes them over?"
- "For each platform you're on: in one sentence, what is its job? If a platform has no job, we cut it or park it."
- "How should content effort split between your products and your services?" (Only if they sell both.)

Then set the intent mix. Explain it: "Every post will have one of three intents: Educate (teach something your audience cares about), Engage (start conversations, be relatable), or Promote (sell directly). Feeds that only promote get ignored, so we set a ratio. For a business like yours I'd start at [X/Y/Z]. Feel right?" See pillar-patterns.md for ratio defaults by business type.

Also capture: 2 or 3 metrics that map to the objectives, and 1 or 2 vanity metrics to explicitly ignore (follower count is the usual suspect).

## Phase 5: Pillar generation

Preparation, before presenting anything: reread everything gathered, and read pillar-patterns.md. Draft 5 to 7 candidates that (a) span all three intents, (b) each serve at least one persona, (c) each connect to at least one objective, and (d) sound like this brand, not a template.

Present each candidate as:
> **[Name]** (intent, funnel stage): one-line purpose. Example posts: [two concrete ideas using their products/audience/words].

Then: "Which of these feel like YOU? Which feel like someone else's brand? Anything missing that you're itching to post about?"

Selection rules:
- Land on 3 to 5. If they want more, group topics under broader pillars.
- At least 1, ideally 2, must be non-promotional (Educate and/or Engage). If the user resists ("I just want to sell"), make the case once: promotional content converts the audience that value content builds; without the value content there is no audience to convert. Then respect their call, but record the risk in the final document.
- Assign each selected pillar: share of content (must sum to 100% and respect the intent mix from Phase 4), intent, funnel stage.

## Phase 6: Pillar deep-dive (one pillar per turn)

For each pillar, draft the full block yourself and ask for reactions:
- Purpose (one sentence: what it achieves for the brand)
- Description (2 to 3 sentences)
- 5 to 10 recurring topics (pull from Phase 2 customer questions wherever possible)
- Formats that fit, per platform (respect Phase 3 capacity)
- Personas served
- 3 to 5 example post ideas, specific enough to make tomorrow
- One success metric (aligned to the pillar's intent: Educate → saves/shares/follows, Engage → comments/replies, Promote → clicks/DMs/conversions)

Ask after each: "What would you cut, and what's missing?" Two focused corrections beat ten polite nods.

## Phase 7: Stress test

Run the four tests from SKILL.md. Present findings as a short list of tensions with proposed fixes, not as questions. Example: "The Engage pillar has no metric a business owner would care about; I suggest tracking profile visits from those posts. OK?"

## Phase 8: Output

Read output-templates.md and use it as a menu, not a template. Include a section only when the interview produced real content for it; leave out the rest, headings included. No "[TBD]" left in the document. An assumption the user accepted goes under Risks and assumptions with a revisit date. Offer a final pass: "Read it once as yourself and once as your best customer. Flag anything that rings false."
