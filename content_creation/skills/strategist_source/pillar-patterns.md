# Pillar Patterns

Reference material for Phase 4 (intent mix) and Phase 5 (pillar generation). These are starting points to adapt, never defaults to copy. A pillar that could belong to any brand in the industry is a draft, not a pillar.

## The three intents

| Intent | What it does | Why the audience tolerates it | Typical metrics |
|---|---|---|---|
| Educate | Teaches knowledge adjacent to what the brand sells | It makes their life better whether or not they buy | Saves, shares, follows, watch time |
| Engage | Starts conversations, entertains, makes the brand feel human | It's enjoyable and relatable | Comments, replies, story interactions |
| Promote | Sells products/services directly: offers, proof, launches | Sparingly used, and trust was already built | Clicks, DMs, bookings, conversions |

The Educate pillar teaches the domain AROUND the offer, not just the offer. An accountant's education pillar is personal finance and tax literacy, not "how our bookkeeping package works." A skincare brand teaches skin science, not just its own ingredients. This is what makes the brand worth following before the audience is ready to buy.

## Intent mix defaults by business type

Propose, then adjust with the user:

| Business type | Educate | Engage | Promote | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Service business (agency, accountant, clinic, coach) | 45% | 25% | 30% | Expertise is the product; education IS the proof |
| E-commerce / product brand | 30% | 35% | 35% | Product can appear inside educate/engage posts without being the point |
| Personal brand / creator | 40% | 40% | 20% | The relationship is the asset |
| Local business (cafe, gym, salon) | 25% | 45% | 30% | Community and personality carry local brands |
| B2B / SaaS | 50% | 20% | 30% | Long consideration cycles reward teaching |

Seasonal note: the mix can tilt toward Promote during launches and peak season (e.g., 30/20/50 for a launch month), then must tilt back. Record both the baseline and the launch-mode ratio in the strategy.

## Proven pillar archetypes

Use these as scaffolding when generating candidates. Always rename them in the brand's voice; "Behind the Scenes" is an archetype, "Inside the Kitchen" is a pillar.

**Educate archetypes**
- *The Teacher*: how-tos, explainers, myth-busting in the domain around the offer.
- *The Translator*: makes jargon, news, or regulation digestible for the audience (great when the domain changes often: tax, tech, health).
- *The Mistake Catalog*: common errors the audience makes and how to avoid them. High save rates.

**Engage archetypes**
- *Behind the Scenes*: the human process behind the product/service. Builds trust cheaply.
- *The Conversation Starter*: polls, "hot take", this-or-that, questions. Explicitly designed for comments.
- *The Relatable Mirror*: the audience's daily struggles and small wins, told with humor or empathy.
- *Community Spotlight*: customers, UGC, testimonials-as-stories (note: a testimonial framed as a story engages; framed as an ad it promotes; decide which pillar it lives in).

**Promote archetypes**
- *Proof & Results*: case studies, before/after, numbers. The most watchable form of selling.
- *The Offer*: launches, promos, seasonal pushes, clear CTAs.
- *Product in Action*: demos, use cases, features framed as outcomes.

**Hybrid archetype**
- *The Point of View*: opinions and stances on the industry (part Educate, part Engage). This is often the differentiation pillar; two competitors can share topics but never a POV. If the differentiation test in Phase 7 fails, this is usually the fix.

## Worked examples (compressed)

**Solo accountant, service business, Instagram + LinkedIn:**
1. Money Made Simple (Educate 40%): personal finance and tax literacy for freelancers.
2. Deadline Diaries (Engage 25%): relatable freelancer-finance moments, polls, tax-season humor.
3. Ask an Accountant (Engage/Educate 15%): answering real audience questions, from Phase 2 FAQ mining.
4. Client Wins (Promote 20%): anonymized case studies plus service CTAs.

**Skincare brand, e-commerce, Instagram + TikTok:**
1. Skin School (Educate 30%): ingredient science, routine building, myth-busting.
2. Real Skin Club (Engage 35%): unfiltered skin stories, community prompts, trends.
3. The Lab (Promote/Educate 15%): how products are made, ingredient sourcing.
4. Shelf Picks (Promote 20%): launches, offers, routines featuring products.

## Anti-patterns to catch

- **Topics masquerading as pillars.** "Tax tips" and "Bookkeeping tips" are two topics inside one Educate pillar. If two pillars share intent, audience, and metric, merge them.
- **The everything pillar.** A pillar covering >50% of content share usually hides 2 or 3 real pillars. Split it.
- **The orphan pillar.** No persona served, or no objective connected: cut it, however fun it sounds.
- **Ratio theater.** An 80% Promote mix labeled as "balanced" because one meme goes out monthly. The mix must describe the actual planned calendar.
- **The capacity mismatch.** Four video-first pillars for a user who can produce one video a week. Format ambitions must fit Phase 3 capacity.
