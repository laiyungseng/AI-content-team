# Writing the insights

The page computes every number on its own. Your job in the analysis pass is
the part arithmetic cannot reach: what the winning posts have in common that
the losing ones don't. That lives in the captions, the hooks, the posting
times and the formats - things sitting in the data you pulled but invisible
to a median.

So read the captions before writing a word. Sort by outlier score, put the
top five next to the bottom five, and look for what actually separates them.
The insight is in the contrast.

## The rule that matters

**Every claim points at posts on the page.** If you cannot name the posts
that support a sentence, cut the sentence. Someone reading this dashboard
should be able to click through and see what you meant.

This rules out the failure mode these strings exist to fix - confident prose
about things you did not observe:

- ❌ "Your audience responds to authenticity." Not visible in any field.
- ❌ "The algorithm favors longer watch time." You have no watch-time data.
- ❌ "Post more consistently." True of everyone, learned from nothing.
- ✅ "Both winners opened on a specific number in the first line. The four
  that missed opened on a question."
- ✅ "The three high-reach, low-engagement posts all ended on 'let me know
  what you think'. The two that converted ended on one instruction."

The difference is not tone. It is whether the sentence would change if the
data were different.

## Scope and honesty

Insights are frozen at build time and shown only on the full pulled window,
so write them against all the posts, not a subset you found interesting.

With fewer than about five posts in a bucket, describe what you see and stop
- "both of these are talking-head cuts" is fine, "you should stop making
talking-head cuts" is not something two posts can support. Say so plainly
when the honest finding is that nothing separates the groups; a dashboard
that admits a flat result stays trustworthy on the weeks it finds something.

Never restate the number printed next to the sentence. The card already says
six posts landed there.

## Length and voice

One to three sentences per quadrant. One paragraph for the headline. Second
person, plain words, and end on something the creator can act on this week -
a format to repeat, a hook to drop, a platform to favor. The headline is the
one place to synthesize across platforms and topics, and it is the first
thing anyone reads, so make it the single most useful thing you found.
